# Tibetan Months

A Tibetan **month** (`zla ba` ཟླ་བ་; Ch. yuè 月) runs from new moon to new
moon and is astronomically defined by the sign of the zodiac the Sun enters
during it. A common year has twelve months; an **intercalary month** (`zla
bshol` ཟླ་བཤོལ་ / `zla lhag pa` ཟླ་ལྷག་པ་; Ch. rùnyuè 闰月) is inserted
when a month passes without a solar sign change.

## Three Naming Systems

Tibetan months are named in three overlapping ways, all attested in the
sources:

1. **Kālacakra (mansion) names** - each month is named after the lunar
   mansion (`rgyu skar` རྒྱུ་སྐར་) near which its full moon falls, e.g.
   `nag pa`, `sa ga` (see [[tibetan-saga-dawa]]), `chu stod`, `gro bzhin`,
   `khrums stod`, `khrums smad`, `tha skar`, `smin drug`, `mgo`, `rgyal`,
   `mcag`, `dbo`. The Kālacakra year begins with the month *Caitra* (`nag
   pa` ནག་པ་), counted as the third month in the Tibetan civil system.
2. **Hor (Mongolian) ordinals** - `hor zla dang po` ཧོར་ཟླ་དང་པོ་ (1st Hor
   month), etc.
3. **Chinese-style animal names** - Tiger, Hare, Dragon, Snake, Horse, Sheep,
   Monkey, Bird, Dog, Pig, Rat, Ox months, using the same twelve animals as
   the `rab byung` རབ་བྱུང་ cycle; see [[tibetan-rabjung-cycle]].

## Month Length and Numbering

- Months contain 29 or 30 **natural days** (`nyin zhag` ཉིན་ཞག་) over which
  the 30 **lunar days** (`tshes` ཚེས་) are distributed; see
  [[tibetan-lunar-phases]].
- The numbering offset between the Kālacakra year (beginning with *Caitra*)
  and the civil year is a known source of tradition-specific differences; the
  kalacakra.org description notes that *Caitra* "should really be the second"
  month but is treated as the third in the Tibetan system.
