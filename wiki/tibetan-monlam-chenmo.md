# Mönlam Chenmo (Great Prayer Festival)

**Mönlam Chenmo** (Tib. `smon lam chen mo` སྨོན་ལམ་ཆེན་མོ་, "the great
prayer [festival]"; Ch. Chuàndà zhāohuì 传大召会 / Mènglán fǎhuì 默朗法会)
is the Great Prayer Festival held in the first Tibetan month, beginning just
after [[tibetan-losar]] and running through the full moon on the 15th.

## History and Observance

The festival is traditionally attributed to Je Tsongkhapa (`rje tsong kha pa`
རྗེ་ཙོང་ཁ་པ་), founder of the Gelug (`dge lugs` དགེ་ལུགས་) tradition, who
instituted it in Lhasa in 1409 CE as a gathering for prayers of aspiration and
the commemoration of the Buddha's display of miracles during the first
fifteen days of the year. This is a historical-religious attribution recorded
in tradition.

During the festival, monks traditionally debated for the `dge bshes` དགེ་བཤེས་
(geshe) degree, and butter-sculpture offerings and processions marked the full
moon (the "Day of Miracles," `cho 'phrul zla ba` ཆོ་འཕྲུལ་ཟླ་བ་ period).
