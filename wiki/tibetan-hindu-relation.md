# Tibetan-Hindu Calendar Relations

The Tibetan calendar is a Tibetan adaptation of the Indian **Kālacakra**
(`dus kyi 'khor lo` དུས་ཀྱི་འཁོར་ལོ་; Ch. shílún 时轮) astronomical system.
It therefore shares deep structure with Hindu (Ch. Yìndùjiào 印度教)
lunisolar calendars, but it is **not identical** to any living Hindu civil
calendar such as Vikram or Śaka Samvat.

## Shared Indian Framework

| Concept | Tibetan (Wylie) | Tibetan script | Sanskrit | Chinese | Notes |
|---------|-----------------|----------------|----------|---------|-------|
| lunar day | `tshes` | ཚེས་ | *tithi* | — | 12° Moon-Sun elongation; see [[tibetan-lunar-phases]]. |
| lunar mansion | `rgyu skar` | རྒྱུ་སྐར་ | *nakṣatra* | xiù 宿 | 27/28 mansions; months named by full-moon mansion. |
| yoga | `sbyor ba` | སྦྱོར་བ་ | *yoga* | — | Combined Sun+Moon longitude periods. |
| karaṇa | `byed pa` | བྱེད་པ་ | *karaṇa* | — | Half-tithi units. |
| five components | `yan lag lnga` / `lnga bsdus` | ཡན་ལག་ལྔ་ / ལྔ་བསྡུས་ | *pañcāṅga* | wǔzhī 五支 | See [[tibetan-pancanga]]. |

- Month names such as `sa ga` ས་ག་ ([[tibetan-saga-dawa]]) and `nag pa`
  ནག་པ་ (Caitra) correspond to Indian mansion-derived month names.
- The Kālacakra year begins with the month *Caitra*, as in several Indian
  lunisolar traditions; the Tibetan civil year numbers it differently (see
  [[tibetan-months]]).

## Key Differences

- **Omitted and repeated dates** (`chad` ཆད་, `lhag` ལྷག་) in the civil
  mapping of `tshes` onto `nyin zhag` are characteristic of the Tibetan
  implementation.
- **Intercalation rules** (`zla bshol` ཟླ་བཤོལ་) differ from the Vikram or
  Śaka conventions.
- **Epochs and later traditions** differ: Phukpa (`phug pa` ཕུག་པ་) and
  Tsurphu (`mtshur phu` མཚུར་ཕུ་) calculations further modify the Kālacakra
  base.
- **Symbolic layer**: Tibetan calendars add Chinese-derived element-animal
  content absent from Hindu calendars (see [[tibetan-chinese-relation]]).

## Summary

The relationship is one of **shared Indian astronomical heritage** - the
pañcāṅga, nakṣatra month names, tithi-style lunar days - not calendar
identity. A Tibetan date cannot be read directly from a Hindu calendar date.
